MUST KNOW
🎒

What stays with you

One habit makes travel days much easier: keep the important things in your day bag, not your main suitcase.

🛂 Passport & travel documents

Know where your passport is at all times, and keep it with you — not packed in your main suitcase — on travel days, including the day of your inter-island flight from Queenstown to Auckland.

It is also good habit to carry some form of ID on your person day to day.

✅ Day bag essentials

Your main suitcase goes into the coach hold on travel days and may not be accessible until you reach the hotel. Keep anything you might need during the day with you:

  • Glasses, contact lenses or hearing aids
  • Medication and prescription items
  • Phone, charger and any cables
  • Cards, cash and travel money
  • A reusable water bottle — we'll have rest stops to refill along the way

A useful rule: if you would miss it for a few hours, it belongs in your day bag.

Entry & visa requirements can vary depending on your nationality. Your AAT Kings documents include guidance — I also recommend checking your government's official travel advice before departure to be safe.
GOOD TO KNOW
💡

Before you pack

These are the details that tend to make the biggest practical difference.

👟 Walking shoes

Lakeside paths, Rotorua's geothermal walkways, suspended treetop bridges, glowworm caves — this journey covers a lot of ground on foot, across both islands.

Comfortable, well-worn shoes make a genuine difference. If you are unsure which pair to bring, bring your most comfortable ones.

🌦️ Weather and layers

New Zealand's weather shifts with the region, the island and the altitude — a mild coastal morning and an alpine afternoon can feel like different seasons entirely.

A waterproof jacket you trust, a mid-layer, and comfortable shoes will do most of the heavy lifting. Forecasts are useful but treat them as a guide rather than a guarantee.

🌦️ My tip: layers beat one big coat every time here — you can add or peel off as the day, and the island, changes.

🔌 Power adapters

New Zealand uses the Type I plug (angled three-pin), the same as Australia — so bring a suitable adapter if your devices use a different plug.

🧳 Packing

Pack for usefulness rather than volume. Most people bring more than they use — and on a tour with several hotel changes, lighter luggage is noticeably easier to live with. Laundry facilities are available at most of the places we stay, so you don't need to pack for the whole trip at once.

If hot pools or thermal spas appeal — Rotorua is the place for it — a swimsuit is worth packing.

Luggage allowance: 1 suitcase up to 23kg / 50lbs, plus 1 soft hand-luggage bag. The hand bag must be soft-sided with no wheels or telescopic handle — space on the coach is limited and soft bags fit much more easily. Label both bags clearly before leaving home. Your tour includes an inter-island flight, and any airline excess baggage charges are the responsibility of the guest. Suitcase delivery to your room can take around 30 minutes after check-in — keep toiletries, medications or anything you need on arrival in your hand luggage.
TOUR RHYTHM
🚌

The rhythm of it

This is a perfectly paced week across both islands — 7 days from Queenstown to Auckland, with an inter-island flight on Day 4 included in your tour. Whatever the day brings, you're in for a real taste of the best of New Zealand.

In the South Island, Queenstown is your base for cruising Lake Wakatipu aboard the TSS Earnslaw steamship and a taste of rural life at Real Country and Walter Peak. In the North Island, the glowworm caves, the geothermal wonders and Māori culture of Rotorua, and a close encounter with a kiwi at the Kiwi Hatchery bring it all together.

This itinerary is designed to let you experience the best of New Zealand — the pace moves, and that's part of what makes it feel so full. There will be some earlier starts and longer travel days, but most people settle into the rhythm surprisingly quickly. Free time is built in, so there's plenty of room to explore at your own pace or add an optional experience.

🚌 On the coach

Seat rotation happens daily, and I'll explain the system clearly on Day One.

Snacks are absolutely fine. Just avoid hot food, hot drinks and anything likely to melt, spill or perfume the entire coach. Please keep your seat area tidy — it's a shared space and it makes a real difference to everyone's day.

Seatbelts on whenever you are seated — it's a simple habit that matters.

🏨 Your hotels

We stay in two modern hotels on this journey, comfortable bases for exploring.

Porterage is included at both, so your bags will be taken care of.

🚗 Our Driver

Our Driver navigates a genuinely varied route — coastal roads, mountain passes and city centres across the country all take real skill.

By the end of the tour, most groups have a real appreciation for just how much goes into it.

⏰ Tour timings & inclusions

Our days run to a schedule. Being ready on time — for departures, meals and activities — keeps things on track for everyone in the group.

I'll always let you know what's coming up and what to be prepared for. All we ask is that you're there on time, ready to go.

LATER ON TOUR
⭐

A few practical things for later

You do not need to make all of these decisions before we meet. They become much easier once you can see the pace of the tour and what suits you.

⭐ Optional experiences

New Zealand has one of the widest ranges of optional experiences I take guests through, and there's usually a selection to choose from in places like Queenstown and Rotorua, depending on where we are.

In my experience, the optionals are often what people remember most. I'll walk you through what's available as we go, and payment is by card only, arranged through me on tour.

💳 Money & cards

Cards are widely accepted across New Zealand, but a small amount of cash can still be useful for smaller cafes, markets or local purchases.

It's a single currency for the whole trip — New Zealand Dollars (NZD) — which keeps things simple.

💶 Gratuities

When you're out during free time, if the service is good — tip. A little local currency at the end of a good meal is always appreciated.

On the tour itself, porterage and restaurant tips on included meals are already taken care of. Tips for your Travel Director and Driver are separate. Guidance on suggested amounts is in your AAT Kings documents, and you can always ask me on tour. This does not need to be solved on the first morning.
